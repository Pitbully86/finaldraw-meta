1. Install FinalDraw

Download the installer Grab FinalDraw-Setup.exe from the download link on your receipt or from the website. It is digitally signed by the publisher.
Run it Double-click and follow the prompts. It installs for your Windows user only — no administrator password needed, no IT ticket.
Launch from the Start Menu Search for “FinalDraw.” First launch offers a guided tour and a sample claim — both worth two minutes.
If Windows shows a blue “Windows protected your PC” screen, click More info → Run anyway. That screen appears for newly published software until Microsoft has seen enough installs; the installer's digital signature (from the publisher) is intact either way — you can right-click the file → Properties → Digital Signatures to verify it.

2. Activate your license

Unlicensed, FinalDraw is a full working demo: everything functions, but every page of every PDF carries an EVALUATION COPY watermark, so it can't be used for real carrier submissions. A license removes the watermark and stamps your company name on every certificate instead.

Open the license screen Click Activate licence at the top right of the first screen.
Paste whatever you were sent One box takes both kinds: the short license key from your purchase receipt, or the whole -----BEGIN FINALDRAW LICENCE----- block if you were sent one by email. Include the BEGIN and END lines when pasting a block.
Click Activate The app figures out which kind you pasted. Keys are checked online; one key activates one computer. Emailed licenses activate instantly, offline.

The title bar always shows your license state — “Licensed to Your Company” means you're set.

Buying by email instead of the website? The license screen shows your Installation ID (like A1B2-C3D4-E5F6-7890). Send it with your company name and your license comes back locked to that computer.

3. The three-step flow

Everything in FinalDraw happens in three steps, shown in the sidebar:

  • Scope — open the carrier's estimate PDF (or start blank).
  • Review — check what was read, fix anything flagged, watch the Money Check turn green.
  • Certificate — generate the PDF, collect signatures, send it.

New here? Click Show me around on the first screen for the guided tour, or Load sample estimate to run a fictional wind/hail claim end-to-end before trusting it with a real one.

4. Step 1 — Open an estimate

Drag the carrier's estimate PDF onto the big drop zone, or click Browse for a PDF…. FinalDraw reads the insured's name, property address, claim and policy numbers, dates, and every dollar figure it can find, so you're checking numbers instead of typing them.

What it reads best

Estimates in the standard Xactimate layout — which is what nearly every major carrier sends. Some carrier formats read completely; others read partially and flag what needs a look. It never guesses silently.

Scanned or photographed estimates

If the PDF is a scan (a picture of paper, no real text inside), FinalDraw tells you and opens the blank form instead — type the fields off the paper. The math checking below works exactly the same.

No PDF at all?

Click No estimate handy — enter the fields myself at the bottom and fill the form in manually.

5. Step 2 — Review the fields

Every field FinalDraw read is shown for review. Two things to know:

  • Amber “verify” flags mean the app is not fully confident about that field — it found something, but you should eyeball it against the estimate before generating.
  • Amount Paid to Date and Remaining Balance are read-only. They are computed from the other figures (that's the whole point — they can't be typo'd).

It learns your carriers

When you correct a field and generate, FinalDraw remembers the correction for that carrier. Next State Farm estimate you open, your fix is applied automatically and marked so you can see it happened.

6. The Money Check, explained

The dark panel at the bottom of the Review screen is the reason FinalDraw exists. It shows the whole payment equation and refuses to look right unless the figures actually reconcile:

FigureWhat it means
RCVReplacement Cost Value — the full approved amount.
ACVActual Cash Value — what the carrier paid up front. ACV already includes the deductible and excludes recoverable depreciation. Double-counting the deductible is the #1 reason carriers bounce certificates.
DepreciationRecoverable depreciation = RCV − ACV. This is the money the certificate releases.
Code / PWICode-upgrade items the carrier pays only when incurred. They sit outside depreciation, on their own line.
Balance due from carrierRCV − ACV + PWI — depreciation plus any code items. The number turns green when everything reconciles.

If the depreciation you entered doesn't equal RCV − ACV, the panel flags it in red and explains which figure to re-check. Fix the numbers until it reads ✓ Reconciles — then generate.

ACV policies (no replacement cost)

Some policies — common with farm-bureau and older homeowner policies — settle on Actual Cash Value only: the depreciation is non-recoverable and the carrier never pays it, so there is no final draw waiting on a certificate. FinalDraw reads this straight off the estimate (Xactimate prints it as “Less Non-recoverable Depreciation <1,060.97>” — angle brackets, not parentheses). When it finds one, it shows an amber ACV policy notice above the scope panel quoting the estimate's own summary lines as proof, the Money Check turns amber and relabels the total “Non-recoverable depreciation (not owed),” and the certificate's wording changes so it never claims a balance is due. Nothing else changes — you can still generate, sign and send; the certificate simply says what is true.

7. Partial scope — certify only the work you performed

Did the roof, but not the siding or gutters? Certifying the whole claim asserts completion of work you didn't do. Instead:

Click “Choose scope” in the Scope panel FinalDraw shows every line item it read off the estimate, grouped by section (Roof, Siding, Gutters…), each with its own RCV, depreciation and ACV.
Tick what you performed Tick whole sections or individual items. The certified figures are recalculated from exactly those items — real addition, not percentages.
Apply The certificate now names its scope, lists the certified work, and the money fields reflect only your items. Clearing the scope puts the whole-claim figures back.
The safety gate: before offering any line items, FinalDraw adds them all up and compares against the estimate's own stated totals. If they don't match to the penny, something was misread and the item picker refuses to hand you figures — enter the scope numbers manually instead. You will never bill from a bad parse.

Code-upgrade (PWI) items are shown greyed in the picker: the estimate accounts for them separately, so they're never part of a scope subtotal. If none of your chosen sections carry code items, the claim's PWI is removed from your certificate — those code dollars belong to another trade's work.

8. Declined work & the acknowledgement

When a homeowner keeps the ACV money and skips part of the scope — takes the interior painting money and spends it on the deck — two things are true that they usually don't know: the withheld depreciation on that work is forfeited, and a future claim on the never-repaired damage will likely be denied.

In the scope picker, mark those items Declined instead of leaving them unticked. When you generate, FinalDraw produces a second one-page PDF alongside the certificate: the Acknowledgement of Declined Repairs. It lists the declined items, the dollars being forfeited, and the consequences in plain words, with a signature line for the homeowner.

Have it signed. It protects both sides: the homeowner gets an informed choice in writing, and you get proof you certified only what you built and told them the rest.

9. Templates & branding

On the Review screen, the Certificate template picker controls how the PDF looks and reads. Click Edit templates… to open the editor:

  • Logo — your company logo on the certificate header.
  • Colors and fonts — match your brand.
  • Wording — the certification paragraph, add or remove rows, rename labels. Save as many named templates as you want (one per carrier, one for mortgage companies, one plain).
  • Preview — see the certificate before saving.

The built-in Default template can't be edited — it's your known-good fallback.

Changing the template after capturing signatures clears them — the signature covers specific wording, so new wording means signing again. The app tells you when this happens.

10. Signatures in person

For the homeowner standing next to you: on the Review screen, click Sign as Homeowner… (or Contractor). They sign the pad with a mouse, finger or stylus, type their legal name, and tick the consent box. The signature is embedded in the certificate with a full audit record — who, when, and a fingerprint of the exact claim values being signed.

If any money figure changes after signing, the signatures clear automatically. A signature on numbers that later changed is worthless; FinalDraw won't let that happen by accident.

11. E-signature setup

For homeowners who aren't in the room, FinalDraw emails the certificate out for legally binding e-signature through your own e-signature account. Bring your own provider means the envelopes bill to your account, your branding is on the emails, and your documents never pass through anyone else's hands.

Click E-signature… on the first screen, pick your provider, and fill in the credentials. They're encrypted with Windows' own protection (DPAPI) and never leave your PC. Use Test connection before saving — it tells you immediately whether the credentials work.

SignWell (easiest start — free tier)

  1. Create an account at signwell.com. Free tier covers 3 documents a month; paid from ~$10/month.
  2. In SignWell: Settings → API → copy the API key.
  3. In FinalDraw: choose SignWell, paste the key, Test connection, Save.

BoldSign

  1. Create an account at boldsign.com (from ~$30/month for 50 documents — the value pick above ~40 docs/month).
  2. In BoldSign: Settings → API → API Key → copy it.
  3. In FinalDraw: choose BoldSign, paste, Test connection, Save.

Dropbox Sign (formerly HelloSign)

  1. Create an account at sign.dropbox.com (API plans from ~$100/month).
  2. In Dropbox Sign: Settings → API → API Key → copy it.
  3. In FinalDraw: choose Dropbox Sign, paste, Test connection, Save.

Zoho Sign (four values instead of one — five extra minutes)

  1. In FinalDraw, set Data centre to the region your Zoho account lives in (com for the US).
  2. Go to the Zoho API Console (api-console.zoho.com) → Add Client → Self Client. Copy the Client ID and Client secret into FinalDraw.
  3. Still in the Self Client, open the Generate Code tab. Scope: ZohoSign.documents.ALL. Generate the code, then exchange it for a refresh token (the console walks you through it). Paste the refresh token into FinalDraw — it stays valid until you revoke it.
  4. Test connection, Save.

12. Sending & tracking e-signatures

Generate the certificate Finish the Review screen and click Generate certificate.
Click “Email for signature” On the success screen. Confirm the homeowner's name and email address, add a message if you like, and send. The envelope goes out from your provider account with a signature box placed for each signer.
Track it in History The History screen shows a Signature column for every sent certificate: sent → awaiting signature → signed. Click Check signature status any time; when it's signed, FinalDraw downloads the executed copy right next to your original, named …-signed.pdf.

13. Email setup — no server names required

FinalDraw sends certificates two ways, and picks the best one available automatically:

If you use classic Outlook (the desktop program)

No setup at all. FinalDraw opens a ready-to-send message in your Outlook — addressed, subject filled, certificate attached. You read it and press Send yourself. Nothing ever goes out without you seeing it.

Everyone else: pick your provider, paste one password

Open Email settings The first time you try to email a certificate without Outlook, FinalDraw offers to set this up. Pick your provider by name: Gmail, Outlook.com, Yahoo, Microsoft 365, or Other. The server details fill themselves in.
Get an app password Gmail, Yahoo and Outlook.com don't allow your normal password in apps — they issue a special “app password” instead. FinalDraw links you straight to the right page for your provider (for Gmail it's myaccount.google.com/apppasswords — requires 2-step verification to be on). Create one named “FinalDraw” and paste it in. Spaces are fine; they're cleaned up automatically.
Send a test The settings screen can send a test message to yourself. If it arrives, you're done — forever. FinalDraw remembers everything (the password is encrypted on your PC).

14. Emailing the carrier

After generating, click Email to carrier. FinalDraw drafts the whole email: the certificate attached, a clean claim summary as the body, and — this is the good part — the To: address usually already filled in. Where does it come from?

  • Off the estimate itself. Carriers print their claims email on their own documents; FinalDraw finds it while reading the estimate. An address from this claim's own paperwork is the most reliable one there is.
  • From the carrier directory. FinalDraw ships knowing some carriers' claims addresses and learns yours as you go — send to a carrier once, tick “remember,” and it's filled in on every future claim with them. The directory is editable under the History screen if an address changes.

Addresses that haven't been confirmed yet are marked so you know to double-check the first time. Review the draft, press send.

15. Attachments — one PDF to the carrier

Adjusters lose separate files. On the Review screen, use Attach documents… to add the carrier's estimate, your final invoice, and job photos. They're appended to the certificate as additional pages — photos get captions — so the carrier receives one PDF with everything in it. Anything unsupported is skipped and reported, never silently dropped.

16. Batch generation

End of the storm season with a folder of finished claims? Click Batch generate… on the first screen:

  1. Point at the folder of estimate PDFs.
  2. Choose where certificates should be saved.
  3. Run it. Every estimate that reads cleanly and reconciles becomes a certificate.

Anything that doesn't reconcile, or is missing an insured name or claim number, is reported for review instead of generated — batch mode never guesses at money. “Skip claims that already have a certificate” is on by default, so re-running a folder is safe.

17. History & duplicate protection

History (first screen, top right) lists every certificate you've ever generated — searchable by name, claim number, carrier, or address — with the insured, date, balance due, and signature status on every row. From there you can reopen the PDF, check e-signature status, and manage the carrier email directory.

The Filed column answers the question every office asks at month end: did this one actually go to the carrier? Emailing the certificate from FinalDraw records it automatically with the date; for ones you sent through a portal or by mail, select the row and click Mark as filed. Anything still showing “—” in that column is a certificate — and a check — that never went out.

If you start a certificate for a claim that already has one, FinalDraw warns you with the date and file name of the existing one before you generate — and even if you proceed, it never overwrites: new files get a numbered suffix.

18. Updates

FinalDraw checks for updates in the background when it starts. When one is available, a banner appears on the first screen: Install now downloads it, verifies it's genuinely from the publisher, installs, and relaunches — under a minute, license and settings untouched. Later dismisses it until next launch. Updates are free.

19. Moving to a new computer

  1. On the old PC: open the license screen and click Remove licence. This frees your activation.
  2. On the new PC: install FinalDraw, paste the same license key, Activate.

Old computer died before you could remove the license? Reply to your purchase receipt and the activation will be reset for you.

20. Where your data lives

On your PC, and nowhere else. FinalDraw has no cloud, no account, and no telemetry on your claims. Certificates save where you choose. Settings, templates, history and the carrier directory live in %APPDATA%\FinalDraw — include that folder in your normal computer backup and you can restore everything.

The only network traffic: license checks, the update check, and — only when you use them — your own e-signature provider and your own email account.

21. Troubleshooting

“This looks like a scanned document”

The PDF is a picture, not text. Enter the fields manually — the math checks work the same. Ask the carrier or your rep for the emailed PDF version when possible.

The Money Check won't turn green

Read its note — it names the mismatch. The usual culprit: the estimate quotes “net ACV” somewhere, or the deductible got subtracted twice. Remember the rules: ACV already contains the deductible; depreciation must equal RCV − ACV.

The scope picker says “Cannot use”

The line items didn't add up to the estimate's own totals, so FinalDraw refuses to offer them (see section 7). Enter the scope figures manually — and if you can, send the estimate's carrier name to support so the parser learns that format.

“Outlook could not be driven”

You have the new Outlook app, which doesn't allow other programs to compose in it. Use the provider-based email setup instead (section 13) — two minutes, works forever.

Gmail won't give me an app password

App passwords require 2-Step Verification to be on first: myaccount.google.com/security → 2-Step Verification → then the app passwords page will appear.

My license says “deactivated” or “could not verify”

Key-based licenses re-check themselves when online. “Could not verify recently” just means connect to the internet sometime soon. “Deactivated by the publisher” means the license was refunded or revoked — reply to your receipt if that's a surprise.

Something else

Email support with the claim's carrier and a description. Real answers from the people who built it.
